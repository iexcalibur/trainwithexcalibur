Reading the tags

Every line is tagged so you can see what it is for and what is safe to drop.

  • Hip Ext Glute and hip-extension work
  • Joints Ankle, knee or hip resilience work
  • Fascia Elastic, long-chain or release work
  • Optional Drop first when time is short
  • Physio Clear with your physio before loading. Substitutions are on the day.

Two rules that shape everything

  • Before you lift: movement only. Long static holds before heavy sets temporarily reduce strength.
  • After you lift: this is where the 30-second holds go.

One joint per day

Ankle, knee and hip each have a full rotation of work. Doing all three every day is roughly 35 extra minutes, which is not sustainable on top of these sessions. One joint per day covers every gap at about 12 minutes.

Be honest about the clock

Full sessions land at 80–95 minutes. If that does not fit your week, cut the Optional lines first, then the fascia block — not the main lifts and not the joint work. A 60-minute session you actually do beats a 90-minute one you skip.

Stop rule

Nothing here should send pain down your leg. If a movement or a stretch does, that is nerve tension, not muscle tightness — stop that item and flag it. Hamstring stretches, deep hip flexion and the banded hip-flexor march are the usual culprits. Anything your physio has prescribed replaces the equivalent item here.

Running the plan

Make it keep working

Progression

Start at the bottom of each rep range. Add reps week to week. When every set hits the top of the range with clean form, add the smallest weight jump available and drop back to the bottom. Repeat.

Example, an 8–12 lift:

Wk 1   10, 9, 8
Wk 2   11, 10, 9
Wk 3   12, 12, 12
Wk 4   +weight → back to 8–10

Joint and fascia work is different

Don't chase load on the Joints and Fascia lines. Progress those by adding a rep, a few seconds, or better control — tendon and collagen remodel over 6–24 months, not weeks. Consistency beats intensity here.

Keep the exercises

Run the same movements for 12–13 weeks. Change one only if it hurts or the equipment is taken. You improve by getting better at a movement, not by collecting new ones.

Deload

If performance drops across several sessions and fatigue stays high — or roughly every 6–7 weeks — take one week at half the sets or noticeably lighter loads. It is part of the program, not a failure.

If the week is too big

Cut in this order, across every day:

  1. Every Optional line — saves about 10 min a session
  2. The Fascia block, except the skipping
  3. One accessory from the main lift block

Never cut: the first two main lifts, the joint block for that day, or the cool-down stretches on Monday.

Track four things

  • Seven-day average body weight — never a single day
  • Waist measurement, once a week
  • Front, side and back photos every 4 weeks — same lighting, time of day, distance
  • Strength on: pulldown, chest-supported row, incline press, leg press, hip thrust

Non-negotiables

  • Sleep 7–8 h. Recovery is where definition is built, and it is what protects your back.
  • Rest days are training. Never double up two sessions to catch up — push the missed day forward.
  • Don't stack hard cardio next to Monday. 7,000–10,000 steps a day covers the rest.
  • Waist stays lean. Skip heavy loaded side bends — they can thicken the waist and work against the V-taper.

The gate, once more

Every Physio item is worth having and worth waiting for. Adding them before you are cleared risks the one thing that would actually derail 13 weeks — a flare-up that costs three weeks of consistency.
